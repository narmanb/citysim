Getting started

1. Build roads beside your zones. Every developed tile needs an adjacent road.

2. Connect a power plant to the first zone tile with a power line. A developed, powered building passes electricity to adjacent developed buildings. Empty zoning cannot carry it onward. Cross a road gap with a line; lines can run on roads.

3. Build a water pump and power it. A waterfront pump supplies 520 units; an inland well supplies 286. Connect its pipe to the first zone tile. Developed, watered buildings pass water to adjacent developed buildings; empty zones and roads do not. Pipes can run on roads and under power lines.

4. An optional water tower stores up to 180 units of surplus water. Connect it by pipe to a powered pump to fill it. It supplies the same pipe network temporarily when pumping falls short; it does not make water by itself.

5. Residential zoning is parcel-based. A single tap creates a 1×1 house lot. In Paint drag mode, every connected residential stroke is one property: longer/wider parcels support larger homes, garages, estates, or—using Apartment lots—larger apartment buildings. The simulation chooses a varied building that fits the property. Parcels need road access, power, water, and demand.

6. Tap + pan is the default: tap places one tile and drag pans. Switch to Paint drag when you want to draw a multi-tile parcel or long infrastructure stroke. With House lots or Apartment lots, one continuous Paint-drag stroke becomes one property. Tap a tile with Pan selected to inspect its parcel and conditions.

↶ and ↷ undo or redo a whole construction stroke, including its cost. City time keeps running. If the treasury goes negative, Budget offers a $10,000 emergency bond repaid at $150 per month for 80 months.

Rail trains need two stations on the same connected rail line. Drag roads or tracks across a water gap of up to eight tiles with dry land at both ends to build a bridge.
